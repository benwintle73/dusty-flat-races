What do these numbers mean?

Chance (e.g. 27%): how likely the tipster rates the runner to win. Every runner in a race adds up to 100%.

Price (e.g. $3.40): what $1 returns if it wins, your $1 included. Market is the bookies' price (horses, and quick-form races). Nan's price is her own, worked out from her chance, for dogs and harness.

3/4 agree: how many tipsters have the same top pick.

Who’s who at Nan’s table

Nan does her homework. Then she checks the market to see if it’s done its homework too.

Pop trusts the computer. He’s trusted it since it tipped him a $21 winner in 1998. He still brings it up.

Bob gets his tips from a bloke at the pub. The bloke’s wrong a fair bit. Bob remains completely confident in him.

Gran backs the favourite. Every race. Every time. Roughies, she says, are for people who enjoy being disappointed creatively.

The Panel averages everyone’s tips and calls it teamwork. If it goes wrong, they’ll be holding a meeting.

Tap a race to see the field. Tap a runner to see what Nan thinks. She’ll explain her reasoning. She won’t promise to be right.

Where Nan gets her gossip

Horses

Nan checks the prices and results on PuntersEdge, then looks at the form on FormFav. If FormFav has no form, she checks the recent runs on PuntersEdge. If both are having a lie-down, she uses Krok’s prices alone and labels the tip “Price only.” Even Nan knows when she’s working with half a newspaper.

Dogs & harness

Nan looks at each runner’s record, recent runs and box or barrier. Her prices are estimates, not bookmaker odds. She’s a tipster, not a tiny bookie in an apron.

The others

Pop reads FormFav’s predictions. Bob reads Krok’s, including the speed map, which sounds very official when he says it. Gran checks the bookies and backs the favourite. She has a system. It’s the same system every race.

Nan’s record

Nan’s top pick in every horse race—and every race at the dog and harness meetings she follows—is recorded before the jump. After the race, it’s checked against the official result. No changing the tip after the finish. No blaming the jockey. No “that’s the one I meant.”

The profit or loss is worked out using a $1 win stake on each pick and the official win dividend. Scratched runners don’t count. Tips stay on the record whether Nan likes the result or not.
